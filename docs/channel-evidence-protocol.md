# Channel MAPPO evidence protocol — 5 October 2026

This is a prospective evidence collection protocol. It is not a statement that the
method is publishable or superior. The previous 128 development and 512
qualification episodes informed development and are not the final test set.
The published paper's VRX results are not compared numerically to 2D results.

## Frozen deployed-model audit

The channel checkpoint is the existing gate-stable seed-42 best checkpoint
(SHA-256 `29f49f7591978f25d23dc033704e9273f83f5b651d67c7c85434751e6c388cde`).
The reference is the existing `paper-parameters-seed42-20260921/adares1.pth`.
This comparison describes two fixed checkpoints, conditional on their different
training histories; it does not isolate the new architecture's contribution.
Both execute physical thruster commands through `evidence_eval.py` in the same
environment, with independent per-world random streams and identical initial states.

The frozen audit uses 2,048 nominal scenarios starting at seed 2,000,000.
It additionally uses 256 scenarios per agility (1.5, 1.75, 2.25, 2.5, 2.75, 3.0),
team size (2, 4, 5, 6, 8), current multiplier (2, 3), and action delay (1, 2 cycles).
Exact ranges are generated by `evidence_eval.suites('frozen')`.
No model will be changed in response to this audit and then reported on it as new data.

VRX uses 100 paired dock scenarios at seeds 2,100,000–2,100,099, agility 2.25,
three defenders, 60 s horizon, 0.2 s action period, 5 m capture/collision radius,
and 15 m target radius. Execution order within each pair is randomized before
running. Both models use identical initialization, actuator mapping, canonical
attacker obstacle ordering, and observation freshness checks. An isolated ROS
domain and Gazebo partition prevent cross-trial traffic. Infrastructure failures
are retained and diagnosed separately, never counted as task outcomes or replaced
with easier seeds. Gazebo has asynchronous timing; pairing is on initial conditions,
not an assertion of identical subsequent physics. A pilot seed 1,980,000 is excluded.

## Independent matched training

Eight training seeds are fixed: 101, 211, 307, 401, 503, 601, 701, 809.
Each arm starts with a newly randomized network, not a continuation of the selected
seed-42 checkpoint. Within each seed block, arm order is randomized with seed 481516.

| Arm | Factor changed from the full method |
|---|---|
| full | None |
| scalar | One fusion gate shared by both actuators |
| thrusters | Independent gates in physical thruster coordinates |
| no_candidates | Neighbor candidate messages removed |
| mean_relations | Uniform neighbor aggregation |
| no_guidance | Verified team-Q gate supervision disabled |
| independent_guidance | Individual rather than joint gate proposals |
| no_motion | Own and relative motion features removed from the actor |
| no_safety_demonstrations | Corrective demonstration pool removed |

All 72 runs use one million on-policy environment steps, identical critics,
collision cost, optimizer settings, training agility curriculum, and development
model selection. Search branch steps, demonstration collection, gradient updates,
wall time, and checkpoint ancestry are separately accounted for. Equal on-policy
steps do not imply equal total simulator or computation cost.

A single demonstration dataset is generated from the fixed ARBoids reference on
512 training seeds starting at 1,500,000. Successful original trajectories form the
anchor pool. For originally colliding cases, common-thrust gates of 1 and 0 are tried
in that fixed order when the existing closest-approach heuristic triggers. Only
complete successful corrections are retained, with the first successful option
selected. Every arm receives the same dataset. The no-safety arm receives the same
anchor pool only. Each independently initialized actor receives exactly 3,600
supervised updates of size 256, learning rate 0.0003; the ordinary batch comprises
192 anchor and 64 corrective samples if a corrective pool exists. No-safety batches
contain 256 anchor samples. The final supervised iterate is used without model
selection. This shared initialization is an explicit training condition, not part
of the deployed policy and not an uncharged advantage over plain MAPPO.

The subsequent MAPPO actor learning rate is 0.000005. Other numerical settings are
fixed by `evidence_matrix.config_for`. Development evaluation uses the existing
128 scenarios starting at 610,000 every 65,536 steps and at the final step. Selection
minimizes collisions among checkpoints with success at least 0.9375, breaking ties
by success then return; below that floor it maximizes success first. No early stopping
based on performance. Checkpoint hashes, initial network hashes, source manifest,
configuration, dataset hash and training logs are retained. Action traces are sampled
every 100 steps with all task terminals retained to keep storage bounded.

The final matrix nominal test is 2,048 common scenarios starting at 9,000,000, kept
unread until all training jobs finish. All arms use this set. Full, scalar, and
thruster arms also use the generalization suites generated by
`evidence_eval.suites('matrix')`. Reserved evaluation seed intervals are explicitly
excluded from new training resets. Neither code nor hyperparameters may be adapted
to final test outcomes without opening a new experiment and fresh test cohort.

## Analysis and claim boundaries

Primary architecture contrasts are full versus scalar and full versus thrusters on
collision probability. The independent unit is a training seed. Paired differences
are computed within each seed on common scenarios; a two-sided exact sign-flip test
is applied to the eight seed-level differences with Holm correction across the two
primary contrasts. Its symmetry/exchangeability assumption is explicit. A crossed
bootstrap resamples training seeds and shared scenarios to show 95% uncertainty
intervals. Other ablations and generalization conditions are exploratory, with all
effects and intervals reported rather than selectively reporting favorable tests.

Success, collision, target breach, capture, timeout denial, return, closest spacing,
episode duration, integrated squared thrust, actor size, communication payload, and
CPU latency are reported. Integrated squared thrust is a control-effort proxy, not
electrical energy. Return is the sum of per-step mean team rewards, excluding the
reset reward, for every controller. Success includes capture and timeout denial;
both components remain visible. Lower collisions alone cannot establish overall
superiority if breaches increase or success degrades.

For two fixed checkpoints, scenario-paired exact McNemar tests and event counts are
reported separately from training-seed inference. The source paper used five
independent training runs; eight are planned here. This choice does not guarantee
adequate power. Planning simulation under independent normal paired seed differences
and a conservative per-comparison alpha of 0.025 gives about 13%, 32%, 49%, 70%, and
85% power for standardized differences 0.5, 0.8, 1.0, 1.25, and 1.5. These are
sensitivity assumptions, not estimates of the method's true effect. Small effects
will remain inconclusive unless more independent training seeds are collected.

This protocol supplies matched attribution, training variability, held-out
generalization, and frozen-model VRX evidence. It does not by itself reproduce the
paper's alternating learned-attacker curriculum or establish superiority to every
published multi-agent method. Those are separately tracked evidence requirements,
not inferred from APF-attacker performance. A publication claim also requires a
source-verified novelty comparison and a contribution supported by the results.

Study-design and power workflows used the experimental-design and statistical-power
procedures described by Kassis, Agarwal, He, Patel and Brueckner, *Scientific Agent
Skills: A Library of Procedural Knowledge for Research Agents* (2026),
[arXiv:2609.00065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00065), current record verified 5 October 2026.
The target reference is [ARBoids](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18549),
IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, DOI 10.1109/LRA.2026.3662620.
